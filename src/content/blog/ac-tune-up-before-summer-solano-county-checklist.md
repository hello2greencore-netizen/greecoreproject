**Short answer:** the best time for an AC tune-up in Solano County is spring, before the first long hot stretch hits Vacaville, Fairfield, and Dixon. A good tune-up cleans the coils, tests the electrical parts, checks the refrigerant charge, and clears the drain, so small problems get fixed on a mild day instead of during a heat wave. Between visits, you can handle the filter, the thermostat, and the space around the outdoor unit yourself.

## Why Solano County air conditioners need a pre-summer check

Inland Solano County gets real summer heat. Afternoons in Vacaville and Fairfield regularly climb into the 90s, and heat waves can push well past 100. Some evenings the Delta breeze cools things off. Other weeks it doesn't show up, and your AC runs hard for days.

That's when weak parts give out. A capacitor that's been fading all winter, a coil packed with dust and cottonwood fluff, or a slow refrigerant leak might not show on a 75-degree day. On a 105-degree day, they show fast.

A spring check also gets you ahead of the rush. When the first heat wave hits, every HVAC company in the region gets busy at once. Catching problems in March or April means repairs happen on your schedule, not the weather's.

## Your DIY AC tune-up checklist

None of these need tools or opening the unit. Do them in early spring, then keep an eye on things through summer.

1. **Change or clean the filter.** A clogged filter is the most common cause of weak airflow and a struggling system. Check it monthly once you're running the AC every day.
2. **Clear around the outdoor unit.** Trim plants back so there's open space on every side. Rake out leaves, grass clippings, and anything that blew in over the winter.
3. **Rinse gently, from a distance.** A light spray from a garden hose can knock surface dust off the outside of the coil. Never use a pressure washer, and never open the panels.
4. **Test the thermostat.** Switch to cooling, set it a few degrees below room temperature, and make sure the system starts and the air coming out feels cool within a few minutes.
5. **Check the supply vents.** Open the registers, move furniture or rugs off them, and walk the house to feel for rooms with weak airflow.
6. **Look at the condensate line.** Find where it drains outside. If it's dry while the AC runs on a hot day, or you see water where it shouldn't be indoors, the drain may be clogged.
7. **Replace thermostat batteries** if your model uses them. A dead battery can look like a dead air conditioner.

If anything on that list doesn't check out, that's useful information for your technician. Write it down.

## Signs your AC needs more than a tune-up

A tune-up can't fix everything. Watch for these as summer gets going:

- **One room or the upstairs never cools down.** That usually points to ducts, not the equipment. Leaky or undersized ducts are common, and our [duct work](/services/duct-work) page explains what we look for.
- **The system runs nonstop on hot afternoons** and still can't hold the temperature you set.
- **Repairs keep stacking up** on an older unit, especially if it uses R-22 refrigerant, which is no longer produced.
- **Your electric bill jumps** compared with past summers at similar temperatures.
- **New noises,** like grinding, buzzing, or banging from the outdoor unit.

## Common questions

### When should I schedule an AC tune-up in Solano County?

Spring is best, ideally March through May, before the first stretch of 90-plus days. That leaves time to handle any repairs before you really need the cooling.

### How often does an air conditioner need a tune-up?

Once a year is a good baseline for a standalone AC. If you have a heat pump that heats and cools, a visit before summer and another before winter is the better schedule.

### Can I clean my AC coil myself?

You can gently rinse the outside of the outdoor coil with a garden hose. Don't open the unit, remove panels, or use a pressure washer, because the electrical parts aren't safe to handle and the fins bend easily.

### Will a tune-up lower my electric bill?

A clean, properly charged system doesn't have to work as hard to cool your home. We can't promise a specific savings number, but a neglected system almost always costs more to run.
