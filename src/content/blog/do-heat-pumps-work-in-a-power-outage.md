**Short answer:** no. A heat pump runs on electricity, so it stops when the power goes out. The same is true of most gas furnaces, which still need power for the blower, the controls, and the igniter. If outages are a concern for your home, the fix is a backup plan, not a different fuel.

## Why a heat pump stops in an outage

A heat pump has an outdoor compressor, a fan, and an indoor blower or air handler. All of them need electricity. There's no pilot light or manual mode to fall back on.

That's different from a wood stove or a standalone gas fireplace, which can put out heat with no power. But those heat one room, not the whole house.

## What about a gas furnace?

This surprises a lot of homeowners. A typical gas furnace burns gas, but it can't run without power.

- The blower motor that pushes warm air through your ducts is electric.
- The control board and thermostat are electric.
- Modern furnaces use an electric igniter instead of a standing pilot light.

So when the power is out, a gas furnace and a [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) are usually in the same boat. Switching from one to the other doesn't make you more or less exposed to outages. If you're comparing options, our [furnace](/services/furnaces) page covers what a gas system involves.

## Backup options if outages worry you

Public Safety Power Shutoffs and winter storms are real in the North Bay, so it's reasonable to plan ahead. The right answer depends on what you need to keep running.

| Option | What it can do | Things to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portable generator | Powers a few essentials | Needs correct hookup and safe outdoor placement. Not for running a whole-house system. |
| Whole-home battery | Can keep selected circuits running | Sized to the loads you choose. A heat pump is a big load, so the battery and system need to be planned together. |
| Solar plus battery | Can recharge between outages | Only helps if the setup is designed to run during an outage. Many solar systems shut off with the grid unless they have a battery. |
| Small ductless unit | Heats and cools one room on much less power | A [mini split](/services/mini-splits) can be a practical backup room if you have a battery. |
| Wood stove or fireplace | Heats a room with no power | Depends on local rules and your home's setup. |

For a big battery project, a licensed solar or electrical pro should design the backup side. What we can do on the HVAC side is size and select the heat pump so it's not asking for more power than it should.

## How to make a heat pump easier to back up

A few choices make a big difference if you ever do add backup power later:

- **Right-sized equipment.** An oversized system draws more power at startup and cycles harder. We size to your home, looking at insulation, windows, sun exposure, and ducts.
- **Inverter (variable-speed) systems.** These ramp up and down gradually instead of slamming on at full power, which is gentler on a generator or battery.
- **Tight ducts and a well-sealed home.** Less lost heat means the system runs less, so it uses less power during an outage.
- **A clear electrical plan.** If you may add a battery or generator, tell us during the free estimate so the panel work can be planned around it.

## Staying comfortable in a short outage

For an outage of a few hours, most Sonoma and Marin homes hold temperature better than people expect, thanks to our mild climate. A few habits help:

1. Close blinds on sunny windows in summer, and open them in winter.
2. Keep doors and windows shut to hold the air you already have.
3. Close off rooms you aren't using.
4. When power returns, let the system come back on its own. Your thermostat settings will handle the rest.

If your heat pump doesn't restart after power returns, check the thermostat and the breaker, then give us a call.

## Common questions

### Will a heat pump restart on its own after an outage?

Usually yes. Many systems have a short built-in delay before the compressor restarts, which protects it. If it doesn't come back after several minutes, check the thermostat and breaker.

### Can I run a heat pump on a portable generator?

It depends on the generator, the heat pump, and how it's connected. Don't plug a whole-house system into a portable unit on your own. Ask us or a licensed electrician to set it up safely.

### Is a ductless mini split better in an outage?

Not by itself, since it also needs power. But a mini split uses less power than a full ducted system, so it can be a more realistic load to back up with a battery.

### Should I avoid a heat pump because of outages?

Not for that reason alone. A gas furnace needs power too. If outages matter to you, plan backup power for whichever system you choose.
