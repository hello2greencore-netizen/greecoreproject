**Short answer:** sometimes, but not always. A heat pump needs its own dedicated circuit, and your panel has to have the room and capacity to support it. Many North Bay homes can add one with no panel work. Older homes, or homes that already carry a lot of electric load, may need an upgrade first. We check your panel at the free in-home estimate, so you know before you commit.

## What a heat pump asks of your panel

Your electrical panel is the box that splits power from the utility into the circuits in your home. A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) is one more thing drawing from it.

Two things matter:

- **Space.** The panel needs an open slot for a new breaker, or a way to free one up safely.
- **Capacity.** The panel and the service feeding it have to handle the new load on top of everything else running at once, like an oven, dryer, and EV charger.

The outdoor unit and the indoor unit each need proper wiring and a disconnect, installed to code and inspected under the permit.

## Why older homes are more likely to need work

Plenty of North Bay homes were built decades ago, when a house ran on far less electricity. Gas handled the furnace, water heater, and often the stove and dryer too.

Older panels can be small, crowded, or out of date. Some have no open slots left. Switching from gas heat to a heat pump moves a big job over to electricity, so an older panel is where we look first.

Newer homes, or homes that already had central air conditioning, often have what they need. An existing AC circuit can sometimes be reused or adapted, though we confirm that on site.

## Signs your panel may need attention

Here is what we look for at the estimate:

1. No open breaker slots, or breakers doubled up to squeeze in more circuits.
2. An older panel brand or style that we'd rather not add to.
3. Signs of heat, rust, or damage inside the panel.
4. A lot of existing electric load, such as an EV charger, electric dryer, or electric range.
5. Plans to add more later, like a heat pump water heater, a battery, or solar.

None of these automatically means an upgrade. They tell us what to look at closely.

## What are the options if the panel is full?

An upgrade is not the only answer. Depending on your home, we may be able to:

- **Add a subpanel** to create room for new circuits.
- **Right-size the equipment.** A properly sized system doesn't draw more than the home needs.
- **Use a ductless system.** [Ductless mini splits](/services/mini-splits) can be an efficient fit for a smaller space or an addition, and the electrical requirements are often lighter.
- **Plan the work in phases,** so the panel is handled once, in a way that also covers a future [electric water heater](/services/electric-water-heaters).

If a full panel upgrade is the right call, we tell you why in plain words, and it goes on the written estimate. Nothing shows up as a surprise on install day. That's the Ownership part of how we work: we own the whole job, including the parts behind the wall.

## What does it cost?

The cost depends on your home, so there is no honest one-size price. A new circuit, a subpanel, and a full panel upgrade are very different jobs. We price it to your home after seeing the panel, and it is included in the estimate along with the equipment.

Rebates may be available for heat pumps depending on program funding. We check what's open when we quote your job and handle the paperwork. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

## Common questions

### Do I always need a new panel to install a heat pump?

No. Many homes only need a new dedicated circuit and breaker. We look at the panel at the free estimate and tell you plainly if more is needed.

### Is a panel upgrade included in the heat pump price?

It's part of the written estimate if your home needs it. Because every panel and every home is different, we price it after we've seen yours.

### Will an electrician have to do the panel work?

Panel work is done to code and inspected under the permit. We coordinate the work and the inspection so you only deal with one company.

### Can I get a heat pump if my home has no ducts and an older panel?

Often yes. A ductless mini split can be a good fit, and the electrical load is usually easier on an older panel. We'll check the panel and recommend what makes sense.
