**Short answer:** choose a ducted heat pump if your home already has ducts in decent shape and you want one system quietly serving the whole house. Choose a ductless mini split if you have no ducts, leaky or hard-to-reach ducts, or an addition or room that never feels right. Both are heat pumps that heat and cool, so the real question is how the air gets to your rooms.

## How a ducted heat pump works

A ducted [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) has an outdoor unit and one indoor air handler. The air handler pushes warm or cool air through your existing ductwork to vents in each room, much like a gas furnace and central AC do today.

That makes it a natural swap for many North Bay homes built with forced-air systems. The equipment disappears into a closet, attic, or crawlspace, and you get a clean look with no units on your walls.

The catch is the ducts. A heat pump is only as good as the air it can deliver. If ducts are leaky, undersized, or poorly insulated, you lose comfort and efficiency no matter how good the equipment is.

## How a ductless mini split works

A [mini split](/services/mini-splits) skips the ducts. An outdoor unit connects through a small line set to one or more indoor units mounted in the rooms you want to condition. Each indoor unit heats and cools its own space.

Because there are no ducts to run, installation is often less invasive. That matters in older homes with plaster walls, tight crawlspaces, or no attic access.

## Ducted vs. ductless at a glance

| | Ducted heat pump | Ductless mini split |
|---|---|---|
| Needs ductwork | Yes | No |
| Looks | Vents only, equipment hidden | Indoor unit visible in each room |
| Room-by-room control | Limited without zoning | Built in |
| Best for | Homes with good existing ducts | Older homes, additions, problem rooms |
| Install disruption | Low if ducts are good, higher if they need work | Usually low |

## Which one fits your home?

Here's how we think it through at the kitchen table:

- **Good ducts and a forced-air system now:** ducted is usually the simplest upgrade.
- **No ducts, such as a wall furnace or baseboard heat:** a mini split is often the better fit, since adding ducts can mean a lot of construction.
- **One problem room or a new addition:** a single mini split indoor unit can fix it without touching the rest of the house. If you're chasing a [hot or cold room](/blog/why-is-one-room-always-hot-or-cold), this is a common answer.
- **Ducts that are leaky or poorly placed:** we look at whether [duct work](/services/duct-work) repairs make sense, or whether going ductless is smarter.
- **You want a mix:** some homes do best with a ducted system for the main floor and a mini split for an upstairs room or garage conversion.

Your climate matters too. Inland towns like Santa Rosa and Windsor get long, hot summers, so cooling capacity counts. Coastal Marin runs milder, and a smaller setup may do the job. Either way, we size the system to the home, looking at insulation, windows, sun exposure, and duct condition, not just the label on the old unit.

## Common questions

### Is a ductless mini split as good as a ducted system?

Yes, for the right home. Both use the same heat pump technology. A mini split can outperform a ducted system with leaky ducts, while a ducted system is hard to beat in a home with good ductwork.

### Can I mix ducted and ductless in one house?

Yes. Many homes use a ducted system for the main living areas and a mini split for an addition, bonus room, or a space the ducts don't reach well.

### Do I have to replace my ducts to get a heat pump?

Not always. If your ducts are in reasonable shape, we can often reuse them. We check them at the estimate and tell you plainly if they need repair first.

### Which is quieter?

Both run quietly when installed well. Ducted systems hide the noise in a closet or attic, while mini split indoor units are in the room but run at low sound levels.
