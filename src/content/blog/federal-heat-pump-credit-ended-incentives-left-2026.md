**Short answer:** the federal 25C credit no longer applies to heat pumps or water heaters installed after December 31, 2025. What's left in 2026 are state and local programs, like TECH Clean California, BayREN, Sonoma Clean Power, and MCE, and what each one offers depends on your address and current program funding. The best move is to ask about incentives at the estimate, before the install date, so nothing gets missed.

## What changed with the federal credit

For a few years, a lot of homeowners counted on a federal credit at tax time when they put in a heat pump or a heat pump water heater. That ended. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025, so it isn't part of the math for a 2026 project.

That changes how people budget. The money you might have gotten back in April is gone. What's still on the table is mostly handled before or during the job, through programs that run in California and here in the North Bay.

The good news: the reasons to switch haven't changed. A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) still heats and cools with one system, and it still makes sense in our mild winters and hot inland summers.

## Heat pump incentives still available in 2026

Here's the short list of programs we check for homeowners in Sonoma, Marin, Napa, and Solano counties. Each has its own rules, and funding opens and closes during the year.

| Program | Who runs it | What it may help with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TECH Clean California | Statewide program | Heat pump heating and cooling, heat pump water heaters |
| BayREN | Bay Area regional network | Home energy upgrades, including electric equipment |
| Sonoma Clean Power | Electric provider for much of Sonoma County | Electric upgrades for its customers |
| MCE | Electric provider for Marin and other Bay Area communities | Electric upgrades for its customers |

A few things to know before you count on any of them:

- **Your address matters.** Which electric provider serves your home decides which local programs you can use.
- **Funding is limited.** When a program's budget is fully reserved, new projects can go on a waitlist or wait for the next round.
- **Order of steps matters.** Some programs need a reservation before the equipment goes in. Installing first and asking later can cost you the incentive.
- **Some programs stack.** Depending on the rules at the time, more than one program may apply to the same project.

We don't put rebate dollar amounts in a blog post, because they change too often to stay accurate. Rebates may be available depending on program funding, and we check what's open for your home when we quote the job. You can see the statewide program's current details on the official [TECH Clean California incentives page](https://techcleanca.com/incentives/).

## Water heaters count too

Incentives aren't only for heating and cooling. [Heat pump water heaters](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) are one of the most common upgrades these programs support, and they're often the easiest first step toward an all-electric home.

If your old gas tank is near the end of its life, it's worth asking about incentives before it fails. A planned swap gives us time to check programs and handle paperwork. An emergency replacement on a cold morning usually doesn't.

## How to plan a heat pump project without the federal credit

Without the federal credit, the plan comes down to a few simple steps:

1. **Start with a free in-home estimate.** We look at your current system, ducts, electrical panel, and the rooms that never feel right.
2. **Size the system to the home.** We size it to your insulation, windows, sun exposure, and ducts, not to the label on the old unit. A right-sized system costs less to run and lasts longer.
3. **Check incentives before scheduling.** We look at which programs are open for your address and tell you plainly if funding is tight.
4. **Get clear, written options.** Heat pump systems are priced to your home, and we show the price and the incentive status separately.
5. **Let us handle the paperwork.** That's the Ownership part of our CORE values. You shouldn't have to chase forms.

For homes without ducts, [ductless mini splits](/services/mini-splits) can be a lower-disruption way to go electric one room or zone at a time, and they may qualify for some of the same programs.

## Is it still worth switching in 2026?

For many North Bay homes, yes. The federal credit was a bonus, not the main reason. A heat pump replaces both an aging furnace and an aging AC, it cools well on 100-degree days in Santa Rosa or Napa, and it heats efficiently through our mild winters.

Whether it pencils out for your house depends on your current equipment, your ducts, your panel, and what local programs are open when you're ready. That's exactly what the estimate is for.

## Common questions

### Can I still claim a federal credit for a heat pump installed in 2026?

No. The federal 25C credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025. Plan around state and local programs instead.

### Which rebates can I get in Sonoma or Marin County?

It depends on your electric provider and current funding. TECH Clean California, BayREN, Sonoma Clean Power, and MCE are the main programs we check, and we'll tell you which ones are open for your address.

### Do I need to apply for rebates myself?

Usually not. Many programs run through enrolled contractors, and Green Core handles the paperwork as part of the job.

### Should I wait for new incentives before replacing my system?

If your system still works well, it's fine to plan ahead. If it's failing, waiting can mean an emergency replacement with no time to check programs, so it's better to get an estimate now.
