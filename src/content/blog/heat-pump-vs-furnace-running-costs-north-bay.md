**Short answer:** in the North Bay, a heat pump is usually cheaper to run than an electric heater and often competitive with a gas furnace, because it moves heat instead of making it. Your actual bill depends on your gas and electric rates, how well your house holds heat, and the condition of your ducts. We don't quote a savings number until we've seen your house and your bills.

## Why running cost is more than the equipment

A gas furnace burns fuel to make heat. A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) uses electricity to move heat from the outside air into your home. Moving heat takes far less energy than creating it, which is why heat pumps are so efficient.

That efficiency matters most in a mild climate like ours. Sonoma and Marin winters rarely stay deeply cold, so a heat pump spends most of the season in its sweet spot. Summer cooling comes along in the same system, which replaces a separate air conditioner.

## What actually drives your monthly bill

Four things matter more than the logo on the equipment:

- **Your utility rates.** Gas and electricity are priced separately and change over time. Ask us to look at your current bills, not a national average.
- **Your house.** Insulation, window quality, and sun exposure decide how much heat you need in the first place.
- **Your ducts.** Leaky or poorly sized ducts waste heat no matter what makes it. Our [duct work](/services/duct-work) team often finds this is the hidden cost.
- **How you use it.** Steady, moderate thermostat settings suit a heat pump better than big temperature swings.

## Heat pump vs. gas furnace at a glance

| | Heat pump | Gas furnace |
|---|---|---|
| How it heats | Moves heat from outdoor air | Burns natural gas |
| Cooling | Yes, same system | No, needs a separate AC |
| Energy source | Electricity | Gas, plus electricity for the fan |
| Best fit | Mild climates, all-electric homes | Homes committed to gas |
| Running cost | Depends on your electric rate and home | Depends on your gas rate and home |

If you keep a [gas furnace](/services/furnaces), a high-efficiency model and sealed ducts still make a real difference. We install both and will tell you honestly which fits your home.

## Can solar or a time-of-use rate change the math?

Yes. If you have rooftop solar, or your utility offers an electric rate plan that suits an all-electric home, a heat pump can cost less to run than it would on a standard plan. If you don't, the comparison leans more heavily on your gas rate.

That's why we ask to see a recent bill of each kind during the free estimate. It lets us talk about your house, not a generic one.

## Rebates and incentives

Depending on your utility and current program funding, rebates may be available for heat pump installs through programs like TECH Clean California, BayREN, and Sonoma Clean Power. We check what's open when we quote your job and handle the paperwork. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

## Common questions

### Will a heat pump lower my energy bill?

It can, but it depends on your rates, your home, and what you're replacing. We'd rather look at your bills and your house than promise a number.

### Is a heat pump cheaper to run than electric baseboard heat?

Generally yes. A heat pump moves heat rather than turning electricity directly into heat, so it uses much less electricity for the same comfort.

### Do I still need a furnace if I have a heat pump?

Most North Bay homes don't. A properly sized heat pump handles our winters on its own. A dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace is an option if you prefer it.

### Do ducts affect running costs?

A lot. Leaky ducts can waste a large share of the heat or cooling you pay for, so we check them on every estimate.
