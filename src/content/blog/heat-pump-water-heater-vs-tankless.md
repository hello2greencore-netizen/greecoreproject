**Short answer:** choose a **heat pump water heater** if you want the lowest monthly bills, rebates, and an all-electric home. Choose a **tankless water heater** if your household never wants to run out of hot water and you plan to stay on gas.

Both are big upgrades over a standard tank. They just solve different problems.

## How each one works

A **heat pump water heater** pulls heat from the surrounding air and moves it into a storage tank, a lot like a refrigerator in reverse. It can be three to four times more efficient than a standard electric tank. We install SANCO2 split systems, with a quiet outdoor heat pump and indoor tank, and GE GeoSpring hybrid tanks.

A **tankless water heater** fires a gas burner only when you open a tap, heating water as it flows through. There's no tank to run empty. We install Navien condensing tankless units, which mount on a wall and vent through PVC.

## Side-by-side

| | Heat pump water heater | Tankless (gas) |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly running cost | Lowest | Moderate |
| Hot water supply | Stored tank, sized to your household | Endless, up to its flow rate |
| Rebates | Often available | Rarely |
| Fuel | All-electric | Natural gas |
| Space | Needs room and airflow, or an outdoor unit | Compact, wall-mounted |
| Install needs | Dedicated electrical circuit | Gas line sizing and venting |
| Maintenance | Clean the air filter, check the anode | Yearly flush |

## When a heat pump water heater makes sense

- You want the lowest operating cost over the life of the unit
- You're working toward an all-electric home, or you have solar
- You want to take advantage of rebates from TECH Clean California, Sonoma Clean Power, MCE, or BayREN (availability depends on location and current funding)
- You have a garage, basement, or outdoor spot for the equipment

Our mild North Bay climate is a good match. Heat pump water heaters work best in spaces that don't get very cold, and SANCO2 systems keep heating water efficiently even on chilly mornings.

## When tankless makes sense

- Your household runs back-to-back showers, a big tub, or several fixtures at once
- You want to free up floor space in a closet or garage
- You have, or can add, enough gas supply for a tankless unit
- You plan to keep gas appliances in the home
