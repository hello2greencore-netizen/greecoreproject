**Short answer:** a heat pump should get a professional check at least once a year, and twice a year is better, once before summer and once before winter. Unlike a furnace or an air conditioner, a heat pump heats and cools, so it runs most of the year. That means more wear, and more reason to keep it clean and checked.

## Why heat pumps need regular service

A gas furnace works in winter. An air conditioner works in summer. A heat pump works in both, so it rarely gets a season off.

Regular service helps in three ways:

- **Efficiency.** Dirty coils and clogged filters make the system work harder for the same comfort.
- **Fewer surprise breakdowns.** Worn electrical parts are easier to catch on a calm spring day than during a heat wave.
- **Longer life.** A well-kept system tends to last longer, and it's easier to stay on top of warranty requirements when you have a record of service.

Think of it like a car. You can skip the oil change for a while, but eventually you pay for it.

## What a heat pump tune-up includes

Every visit is a little different, but a good heat pump check covers the whole system, not just the outdoor unit. At Green Core, that means we:

1. Clean the indoor and outdoor coils.
2. Test the electrical parts, like capacitors and contactors.
3. Confirm the refrigerant charge is right.
4. Check the defrost cycle, which matters in winter.
5. Clear the condensate drain so water leaves the system the way it should.
6. Check airflow, since weak airflow often points to a duct or filter problem.

You get a plain explanation of what we found. If something needs repair, you get a written price before we do any work. If everything looks good, we tell you that too.

## What you can do between visits

A few simple habits make a real difference, and none of them need tools.

- **Check the filter.** Look at it monthly during heavy use and replace or clean it when it's dirty. A clogged filter is the most common cause of weak airflow.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear.** Trim plants back from the sides and keep leaves, grass clippings, and debris off the top. North Bay yards drop plenty of leaves and seed fluff in some seasons.
- **Watch for ice that stays.** Some frost on the outdoor unit in winter is normal, because the system runs a defrost cycle. A unit that stays iced over needs a look.
- **Listen and feel.** New noises, lukewarm air, or a system that turns on and off quickly are all worth a call.

Don't open the unit or hose out the coils yourself. The electrical parts inside are not safe to handle, and a wrong move can damage the fins.

## Does a ductless mini split need service too?

Yes. A [ductless mini split](/services/mini-splits) is a heat pump, so it needs the same attention. The indoor heads have washable filters that you can clean yourself, and the coils and drain lines inside the heads collect dust and moisture over time. A professional check keeps them from getting musty or clogging.

If your system is ducted, the ducts matter as much as the equipment. Leaky or dirty ducts waste a lot of what the heat pump produces. See our [duct work](/services/duct-work) page for what we look at there.

## Common questions

### Is once a year enough for a heat pump?

For many homes, yes, as long as you keep the filters clean. Because a heat pump runs year-round, a visit before summer and another before winter is the better schedule if you want to catch problems early.

### Can I skip service if my heat pump is new?

It's still worth having it checked. Many manufacturers expect regular maintenance as part of keeping the warranty in good standing, so check your paperwork. A visit also confirms that the install is performing the way it should.

### How do I know my heat pump needs a repair, not just a tune-up?

Lukewarm air, ice that does not clear, short cycling, strange noises, or an error code all point to something beyond routine upkeep. Call us and we'll test the system and give you a written price before any repair.

### Is heat pump service different from furnace service?

Somewhat. A furnace check focuses on the burner, igniter, and heat exchanger. A heat pump check focuses on refrigerant, coils, the defrost cycle, and electrical parts, because the system works as both your heating and your cooling.
