**Short answer:** MCE is the community choice electricity provider for most of Marin County, and it runs programs that can help homeowners switch from gas to electric equipment like heat pumps and heat pump water heaters. MCE rebates may be available depending on program funding, and they often stack with programs like BayREN and TECH Clean California. Amounts and rules change, so check MCE's official page before you plan around a number, and let your contractor confirm what's open for your address.

## Who can use MCE rebates?

MCE buys and supplies electricity for its member communities, while PG&E still delivers it and sends the bill. If your PG&E bill lists MCE as your generation provider, you're an MCE customer.

In Marin, that covers most homes in towns like [San Rafael](/service-areas/san-rafael), [Novato](/service-areas/novato), Mill Valley, Tiburon, San Anselmo, and Fairfax. If you opted out and buy power straight from PG&E, MCE's programs may not apply to you. Other programs still might.

Not sure which provider you have? Look at the electric generation section of your PG&E bill. It takes about a minute.

## What MCE rebates can help pay for

MCE's home programs focus on going electric. The equipment that comes up most often in Marin homes:

- **Heat pump water heaters.** This is the most common first step. Swapping an aging [gas water heater](/services/gas-water-heaters) for a [heat pump water heater](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) cuts gas use without touching the rest of the house.
- **Heat pumps for heating and cooling.** A ducted [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) can replace an old furnace and add cooling at the same time, which a lot of Marin homes never had.
- **Ductless mini splits.** For older homes with no ducts, a hillside addition, or a single room that never gets comfortable.
- **Electrical work.** Going electric sometimes needs a new circuit or a panel upgrade. We'll tell you if any program you qualify for helps with that cost.

Some programs offer extra help for income-qualified households. If that might be you, ask about it early, because those programs usually have their own application steps.

Rebate amounts change during the year, so we don't list them here. Check [MCE's rebates and incentives page](https://mcecleanenergy.org/find-rebates-and-incentives/) for the latest, or give us a call and we'll look it up for your address.

## How MCE fits with other programs

MCE isn't the only option for Marin homeowners. Depending on funding and your home, you may also be able to use:

| Program | Who runs it | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| MCE | Marin's community choice provider | MCE electric customers |
| BayREN | Bay Area regional energy network | Homeowners in the nine Bay Area counties |
| TECH Clean California | Statewide program | California homeowners, through enrolled contractors |

Some of these can be combined on the same project, and some can't. The rules shift as funding opens and closes. That's why we check every program that applies when we quote your job. You can read more in our posts on [BayREN rebates](/blog/bayren-home-energy-rebates-sonoma-marin) and [how Green Core handles rebate paperwork](/blog/how-green-core-handles-rebate-paperwork).

One thing that's no longer on the list: the federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

## How to plan an electric upgrade in Marin

Rebate programs reward planning. A few habits make a real difference:

1. **Don't wait for a breakdown if you can help it.** When a water heater fails on a Sunday, the fastest fix is usually a like-for-like replacement. Planning ahead gives you time to go electric and line up rebates.
2. **Get the estimate before you buy anything.** Many programs require the work to be done by a participating contractor, and some need approval before installation.
3. **Pull the permit.** Most rebates require a permitted, inspected install. We handle the permit on every job.
4. **Check your panel early.** Older Marin homes often have smaller electrical panels. Knowing up front avoids surprises on install day.
5. **Keep your paperwork.** Invoices, model numbers, and permit sign-off are what rebate programs ask for. We collect and submit them for you.

Every project is priced to your home after a free in-home estimate. We size the system to the house itself (insulation, windows, sun exposure, ducts), not to the label on the old unit, so you're not paying for more equipment than you need.

## Common questions

### Do I have to be an MCE customer to get MCE rebates?

Usually, yes. MCE programs are generally for customers who get their electricity through MCE. If you opted out to PG&E, programs like BayREN or TECH Clean California may still apply.

### Can I combine MCE rebates with BayREN or TECH Clean California?

Sometimes. Each program sets its own stacking rules, and they change with funding. We check what can be combined for your project before you commit.

### Are there rebates for replacing a gas water heater with a heat pump water heater in Marin?

There often are, through MCE and other programs, depending on current funding. It's one of the most common electric upgrades we install in Marin.
