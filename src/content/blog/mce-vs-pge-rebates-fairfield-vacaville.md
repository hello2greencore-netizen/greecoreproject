**Short answer:** Fairfield is an MCE community and Vacaville is not, so neighbors a few miles apart on I-80 can see different rebates for a heat pump or heat pump water heater. Most Fairfield homes get electricity through MCE and may qualify for MCE programs, while most Vacaville homes buy power straight from PG&E. Statewide and regional programs like TECH Clean California and BayREN can still apply in both cities, depending on current program funding.

## Why Fairfield and Vacaville rebates can differ

It comes down to who supplies your electricity, and that varies across [Solano County](/service-areas/solano-county). MCE is a community choice electricity provider. When a city joins, MCE becomes the default supplier for homes there, and PG&E keeps delivering the power, maintaining the lines, and sending the bill.

As of November 2026, MCE's [Solano County page](https://mcecleanenergy.org/solano-county/) lists Benicia, Fairfield, Vallejo, and unincorporated Solano County as the communities it serves. Vacaville isn't on that list. That's why the same project can line up differently in the two cities.

| | Fairfield | Vacaville |
|---|---|---|
| Default electricity supplier | MCE | PG&E |
| Who delivers power and sends the bill | PG&E | PG&E |
| MCE programs | May apply if you're an MCE customer | Usually don't apply inside city limits |
| TECH Clean California and BayREN | May apply | May apply |

One wrinkle: some homes with a Vacaville or Fairfield mailing address sit outside city limits. Unincorporated areas like Green Valley, Elmira, and Allendale fall under unincorporated Solano County, which MCE serves. So your mailing address alone doesn't settle it.

## How to tell if you're an MCE or PG&E customer

The quickest check is your PG&E bill.

1. **Find the electric generation section.** If it lists MCE, MCE is your supplier.
2. **No MCE line?** You buy generation directly from PG&E. That's typical inside Vacaville city limits, and for Fairfield homes that opted out of MCE.
3. **Keep a recent bill handy.** Rebate programs almost always ask for your account details, and we'll ask to see it at the estimate.

If you moved to Fairfield recently, maybe for a Travis AFB assignment, you were likely enrolled in MCE automatically unless you opted out. Our [Travis AFB HVAC checklist](/blog/travis-afb-fairfield-hvac-checklist-before-you-buy) covers other things worth checking in a new home.

## Rebates that may apply in both cities

Being with PG&E instead of MCE doesn't leave you without options. Two programs don't depend on your electricity supplier:

- **TECH Clean California.** A statewide program that works through participating contractors.
- **BayREN.** A partnership of the nine Bay Area counties, and Solano County is one of them.

Both focus on moving homes from gas to efficient electric equipment. In Solano County homes, that usually means:

- **A heat pump** in place of an aging [gas furnace](/services/furnaces) and central AC. A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) handles Vacaville and Fairfield's 100-degree summer afternoons and cool winter nights with one system.
- **A heat pump water heater** in place of a gas tank. A [heat pump water heater](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) uses far less energy than a standard electric tank and takes gas out of the garage.

Amounts, eligible equipment, and stacking rules change during the year as funding opens and closes. That's why we don't publish dollar figures here. A number in a blog post can go stale long before anyone updates it. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025, so these state and regional programs are the main incentives to plan around now.

## Timing matters more than the city

Most missed rebates come from timing, not from where you live. A few habits help in either city:

- **Bring up rebates at the estimate.** Some programs need a reservation or enrollment before the install.
- **Use a participating contractor.** Several programs only pay out through enrolled contractors.
- **Plan before something breaks.** A same-day replacement on a failed furnace or water heater in July leaves little time to line up incentives.
- **Don't count on a neighbor's number.** What a Fairfield homeowner got last spring may not match what's open for a Vacaville home today.

## Common questions

### Is Vacaville part of MCE?

Not as of November 2026. MCE's Solano County page lists Benicia, Fairfield, Vallejo, and unincorporated Solano County, so most homes inside Vacaville city limits buy electricity from PG&E.

### I live in Fairfield but opted out of MCE. Can I still get rebates?

Possibly. MCE programs are generally for MCE customers, but TECH Clean California and BayREN don't depend on your electricity supplier. We check what's open for your home at the estimate.

### Does switching to MCE change who I call for an outage?

No. PG&E still delivers power and maintains the lines in both Fairfield and Vacaville, so outages and billing still go through PG&E.

### Can I combine rebates from different programs?

Sometimes. Each program sets its own stacking rules, and those change with funding. We check what can be combined for your project before you sign.
