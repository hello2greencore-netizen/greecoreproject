**Short answer:** yes, heat pumps work well for Mill Valley homes, and the hills, shade, and small spaces are mostly things to plan around, not reasons to skip one. A heat pump heats and cools with one system, and a ductless mini split can solve homes that have tight closets, no attic space, or no ducts at all. The right setup depends on how your house is built, so we start with a free in-home look.

## Why a heat pump fits Mill Valley

Mill Valley has a mild, marine-influenced climate. Many homes tuck into hillsides and redwoods, and they often stay cool and shaded even when it's warm elsewhere in the county. That makes a [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) a natural fit. It moves heat instead of burning gas, so it can warm the house on a cool, foggy morning and cool it on the occasional hot stretch.

Plenty of Mill Valley homes were built long before anyone planned for central air. That's not a problem. It just shapes which kind of system makes sense.

## Hills and access

Steep driveways, narrow streets, and stairs to the front door are part of life here. They matter on install day because equipment has to get from the truck to the house.

- **Outdoor unit placement.** The outdoor unit needs a level, stable spot with airflow around it. On a hillside lot, that might mean a pad on a side yard, a wall bracket, or a spot near the existing gas furnace or AC.
- **Moving equipment.** Tight access can mean more hand-carrying and a bit more time. We plan for it when we walk the property, so there are no surprises.
- **Drainage and debris.** Hillside lots collect leaves and needles. We pick a location that's easy to keep clear.

## Shade, trees, and your heating and cooling load

Redwoods and oaks can keep a house cooler in summer, which lowers your cooling needs. They can also mean less winter sun through the windows. When we size a system, we look at the whole home: insulation, windows, sun exposure, and the condition of the ducts. We size to your house, not to the label on the old unit.

Over-sizing is a common mistake in older homes with shade. A system that's too big short-cycles, runs less efficiently, and feels less comfortable. A right-sized one runs longer at a lower speed, which is quieter and steadier.

## Small spaces: ducted or ductless?

Mill Valley homes often have small utility closets, shallow crawlspaces, or cramped attics. That's where the choice between ducted and ductless matters.

| Situation | Often a good fit |
|---|---|
| Existing ducts in decent shape, room for an indoor unit | Ducted [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) |
| Ducts that are leaky, crushed, or too small | Ducted heat pump plus [duct work](/services/duct-work) repairs |
| No ducts, an addition, or a converted garage or studio | [Ductless mini split](/services/mini-splits) |
| A few rooms that never get comfortable | Mini split added to those rooms |

Ductless systems have a slim indoor unit on the wall and a small outdoor unit connected by a thin line set. That's often easier in older homes than cutting new duct runs through finished walls.

## Rebates and incentives

Depending on your utility and current program funding, rebates may be available through programs like TECH Clean California, BayREN, and MCE. Funding changes during the year, so we check what's open when we quote your job and handle the paperwork. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

## Common questions

### Will a heat pump keep my Mill Valley home warm in winter?

Yes, when it's sized correctly and the home is reasonably sealed. Our winters are mild, which is where heat pumps do their best work.

### Can I put a heat pump in a house with no ducts?

Yes. A ductless mini split is built for that situation. We can heat and cool one room or several, depending on your layout.

### Is a hillside lot a problem for the outdoor unit?

Usually not. We look at placement during the free estimate and choose a spot with good airflow and easy access for future service.
