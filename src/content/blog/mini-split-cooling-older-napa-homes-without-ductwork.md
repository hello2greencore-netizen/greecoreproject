**Short answer:** the most practical way to cool an older Napa home without ductwork is usually a ductless mini split. It's a heat pump that sends cool air straight into each room through slim indoor units, connected to an outdoor unit by a small line set, so there's no need to open walls or squeeze ducts into a tight attic. And because it's a heat pump, the same system heats the house in winter.

## Why older Napa homes are hard to cool

Napa has a lot of homes built long before central air was standard. Craftsman bungalows and Victorians near Fuller Park and downtown, and older homes in neighborhoods like Alta Heights, were often heated with a wall [furnace](/services/furnaces) or floor heater and never had cooling at all.

That worked when people opened the windows at night and rode out a few warm days. But Napa afternoons get hot in summer, and fire season can mean keeping the windows shut for days. A box fan in the bedroom only goes so far.

Adding ducts to a house like this isn't always realistic. Plaster walls, low attics, crawlspaces, and finished ceilings leave little room to run them without a lot of cutting and patching. That's where a mini split comes in.

## How a ductless mini split cools a Napa home

A [ductless mini split](/services/mini-splits) has two main parts:

- **An outdoor unit** that sits on a pad or a wall bracket, usually along the side or back of the house.
- **One or more indoor units** in the rooms you want to cool. Each one connects to the outdoor unit through a refrigerant line set and a small drain line, which pass through a hole about the size of a coffee mug.

Each indoor unit has its own controls, so you can cool the bedrooms at night and leave the spare room off. Modern inverter units speed up and slow down instead of slamming on and off, which keeps temperatures steady and runs quietly.

In winter it reverses and heats, the same way any [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) does. For a lot of older Napa homes, that means finally retiring a tired wall furnace, too.

## Mini split options for older homes

There's more than one way to lay out a ductless system. Here's how the common setups compare:

| Setup | What it is | Good fit when |
|---|---|---|
| Single-zone | One outdoor unit, one indoor unit | You need to cool one main space, like a living room or a primary bedroom |
| Multi-zone | One outdoor unit, two or more indoor units | You want bedrooms and living areas each under their own control |
| Several single-zone systems | Separate outdoor units for different parts of the house | The house is spread out, or rooms are far apart |
| Ceiling cassette or slim ducted unit | Indoor unit tucked into the ceiling or a small chase | You'd rather not see a unit on the wall, and there's space to hide one |

Wall-mounted units are the most common and the least invasive. In homes with character worth protecting, we spend time on placement so the indoor units and line sets are as tidy as possible. We can often run lines through a closet, an exterior wall, or under the house instead of across a front facade.

If part of the house already has ducts in decent shape, a ducted heat pump for that section plus a mini split for an addition or upstairs room can make sense. We'll talk through both. Our post on [ducted heat pumps vs. ductless mini splits](/blog/ducted-heat-pump-vs-ductless-mini-split) covers the tradeoffs in more detail.

## Rebates and permits in Napa

Most Napa homes get their electricity through MCE, with PG&E handling delivery. Depending on current program funding, heat pumps, including mini splits, may qualify for rebates through programs like MCE, TECH Clean California, or BayREN. Funding changes during the year, so we check what's open when we quote your job and handle the paperwork. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

Inside city limits, permits go through the City of Napa. Outside city limits, they go through Napa County. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection either way. See our [Napa service area page](/service-areas/napa) for more on how we work in town.

## Common questions

### Can you add air conditioning to an older Napa home with no ducts?

Yes. Ductless mini splits are made for exactly this. We mount compact indoor units in the rooms that need them and connect them to an outdoor unit through a small hole in the wall, so there's no need to open up walls or ceilings.

### Will a mini split keep up on a hot Napa afternoon?

Yes, when it's sized and placed correctly. Modern inverter mini splits are built for hot summer days, and placing indoor units where the heat actually builds up makes a big difference.

### Do mini splits heat too?

Yes. A mini split is a heat pump, so it heats in winter and cools in summer. Many Napa homeowners use one to replace an old wall furnace or space heaters.

### How long does a mini split installation take?

A single-zone system is often done in a day. Multi-zone systems, or jobs that need a new circuit or panel work, can take longer. We give you a clear schedule up front.
