**Short answer:** if your American Canyon home was built in the late 1990s or 2000s and still has its original AC, it's probably due for replacement or close to it. Most air conditioners last around 15 to 20 years, so original systems in these neighborhoods are at or past that point. If yours needs a major repair, struggles to cool the upstairs, or still runs on R-22 refrigerant, replacing it is usually the better value.

## Why so many American Canyon AC units are wearing out at once

A lot of American Canyon grew up fast in the late 1990s and 2000s. Neighborhoods like Vintage Ranch and the streets off Highway 29 went up within a few years of each other, often with similar equipment from the builder.

That means whole blocks of original air conditioners and furnaces are hitting the end of their lives together. If your neighbors are replacing theirs, yours probably isn't far behind.

Builder-grade systems were often picked to hit a price, not to fit each house. Many were sized by rule of thumb, and the ductwork was run quickly in the attic. Twenty years later, that shows up as hot bedrooms, high summer bills, and a system that runs all afternoon.

## Signs your original AC is due for replacement

Age alone doesn't mean you have to replace it today. But if your system is 15 or more years old and you're seeing any of these, it's time to start planning:

- **A major repair.** A failed compressor, a refrigerant leak in the coil, or a dead condenser fan motor on an old system is often the tipping point.
- **R-22 refrigerant.** Systems installed before about 2010 often use R-22, which is no longer produced in the U.S. Recharging one gets more expensive every year.
- **The upstairs never cools.** Two-story American Canyon homes on one system often stay noticeably warmer upstairs on hot afternoons, and an aging system makes it worse.
- **Bills creeping up.** An aging system loses efficiency and runs longer to do the same job.
- **More noise, more cycling.** Rattling, buzzing, or short on-off cycles point to worn parts.
- **Repairs every summer.** If you're calling for service every year, that money is better put toward a new system.

**A simple rule:** if the AC is 15 years or older and the repair is a big one, replacement usually wins. We'll show you both prices and tell you honestly which makes more sense.

## What to replace it with

When the original AC goes, the furnace is usually the same age. That's a good moment to look at the whole system, not just the outdoor unit.

| Option | What it does | Good fit when |
|---|---|---|
| New AC (keep the furnace) | Cools through your existing ducts | The furnace is newer and in good shape |
| New AC and furnace | Replaces both original units | You want to keep gas heat |
| Heat pump | One system that cools in summer and heats in winter | Both units are old, or you want to move away from gas |
| Ductless mini split | Heats and cools individual rooms | One room or a bonus room never gets comfortable |

A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) is a strong fit for many American Canyon homes. Summers are warm but not as extreme as farther up the valley, and winters are mild, which is right in a heat pump's comfort zone. It cools like a central [air conditioner](/services/air-conditioning) and then reverses to heat the house in winter, so you replace two aging units with one.

New systems also use newer refrigerants than the R-22 and R-410A equipment that went into these homes originally, so you're not buying into a refrigerant that's being phased out.

## Get the size and the ducts right this time

The most common replacement mistake is copying the size of the original unit. If the builder's system was sized by rule of thumb, a same-size swap repeats the problem.

We size the new system to the home. On the free in-home estimate we look at insulation, windows, which way the house faces, the layout upstairs and down, and how your ducts are holding up. Afternoon wind off the bay helps some homes and does nothing for a west-facing upstairs bedroom.

Ducts matter just as much. In many 2000s homes, flex duct in the attic has sagged, pulled apart, or been crushed by storage over the years. Leaks mean you're cooling the attic instead of the bedrooms. Sometimes sealing and a few repairs fix it. Sometimes a run needs to be resized so the upstairs actually gets its share. See our [duct work](/services/duct-work) page for more.

Every home is different, so systems are priced to your home after the estimate, with clear written options and no pressure.

## Rebates and permits in American Canyon

American Canyon homes get their electricity through MCE, with PG&E handling delivery. Depending on current program funding, heat pumps may qualify for rebates through programs like MCE, TECH Clean California, or BayREN. Funding changes during the year, so we check what's open when we quote your job and handle the paperwork. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

Permits go through the City of American Canyon. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection. See our [American Canyon service area page](/service-areas/american-canyon) for more on how we work in town.

## Common questions

### How long does an AC last in American Canyon?

Most air conditioners last around 15 to 20 years. Regular maintenance, a clean filter, and healthy ducts help a system reach the longer end of that range.

### Should I replace my AC and furnace at the same time?

If they're both original, usually yes. They were installed together, they'll wear out around the same time, and replacing both at once lets us match the equipment and fix the ducts in one visit.

### Is it worth repairing an R-22 air conditioner?

A small repair can make sense, but a refrigerant leak on an R-22 system is usually the point to replace. R-22 is no longer produced, so recharging keeps getting more expensive.

### How long does an AC replacement take?

Most AC or full system replacements take one to two days on site. Duct replacement or electrical work can add time, and we give you a clear schedule up front.
