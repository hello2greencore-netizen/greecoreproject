**Short answer:** Napa County homeowners can usually look at three programs when they switch to a heat pump or heat pump water heater: MCE, BayREN, and TECH Clean California. Which ones apply depends on your address, your electric provider, and current program funding. The simplest way to avoid missing one is to ask about rebates at the estimate, before anything is installed.

## The three programs Napa County homeowners should know

Each program comes from a different place, so it helps to know who runs what.

| Program | Who runs it | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| MCE | Community choice electricity provider | Homes that get their electricity through MCE |
| BayREN | Partnership of the nine Bay Area counties, including Napa | Bay Area homeowners and renters |
| TECH Clean California | Statewide program | California homeowners, through participating contractors |

None of these replace the others. Depending on the program rules and funding, they can sometimes be combined on the same project. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025, so these state and local programs are what's left to plan around.

## Are you an MCE customer in Napa County?

MCE buys and supplies electricity for its member communities. PG&E still delivers the power, maintains the lines, and sends the bill.

As of November 2026, MCE's [Napa County page](https://mcecleanenergy.org/napa-county) lists the City of Napa, American Canyon, Calistoga, St. Helena, and unincorporated Napa County among the communities it serves. If you live in one of those areas and didn't opt out, you're most likely an MCE customer.

To check, look at the electric generation section of your PG&E bill. If MCE is listed, its programs may apply to you. If you opted out and buy power straight from PG&E, MCE programs usually won't, but BayREN and TECH Clean California still might.

## What these Napa County rebates can help pay for

These programs focus on moving homes from gas to efficient electric equipment. In Napa homes, the projects that come up most are:

- **Heat pumps.** A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) replaces both an aging [furnace](/services/furnaces) and a central air conditioner with one system. That's a good fit for Napa County, where upvalley towns like Calistoga and St. Helena get hot summer afternoons and chilly winter nights.
- **Ductless mini splits.** Older Napa homes and additions often have no ductwork. [Ductless mini splits](/services/mini-splits) heat and cool room by room. We cover this in more detail in [cooling an older Napa home without ductwork](/blog/mini-split-cooling-older-napa-homes-without-ductwork).
- **Heat pump water heaters.** A [heat pump water heater](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) uses far less energy than a standard electric tank and gets gas out of the garage.

Rebate amounts and which equipment qualifies change during the year. That's why we don't put dollar figures in this post. A number in a blog article can go stale long before the page gets updated.

## How to avoid missing a rebate

Most rebate problems come from timing, not eligibility. A few habits make a big difference:

1. **Ask before you sign.** Some programs need to be reserved or enrolled before the install date. Bring it up at the estimate.
2. **Use a participating contractor.** Several programs only pay out through contractors enrolled with them.
3. **Don't wait for an emergency.** If your furnace or water heater is old, plan the switch now. A same-day replacement on a broken system leaves less time to line up incentives.
4. **Keep your PG&E bill handy.** Your account details and generation provider are usually the first things a program asks for.
5. **Expect funding to change.** Programs can open, pause, or change amounts during the year. What a neighbor got last spring may not match what's open today.

## Common questions

### Can I combine MCE, BayREN, and TECH Clean California rebates?

Sometimes. Each program sets its own rules about stacking, and those rules change with funding. We check what can be combined for your project when we put together your estimate.

### Do I need to be an MCE customer to get rebates in Napa County?

Not for every program. MCE programs are generally for MCE customers, but BayREN covers Napa County and TECH Clean California is statewide, so you may still have options if you buy power directly from PG&E.

### Can I apply for a rebate after the install?

It depends on the program. Some require enrollment or a reservation before the work is done, which is why we bring up rebates at the estimate instead of after.

### Is the federal heat pump credit still available?

No. The federal 25C credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025. State and local programs are the main incentives to look at now.
