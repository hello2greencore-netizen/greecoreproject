**Short answer:** yes, most older homes in San Anselmo and Fairfax can be heated without gas. A heat pump, either ducted or ductless, heats in winter and cools in summer using electricity alone. The right choice depends on your ducts, your electrical panel, and how your house is laid out, so we start with a free in-home look.

## Why older Ross Valley homes are a good fit

Many San Anselmo and Fairfax homes are cottages, mid-century houses, and hillside homes tucked under redwoods and oaks. Shade and creek-side dampness keep them cool in winter. Summers are milder than inland Sonoma County, but warm afternoons still happen, and plenty of these homes were built long before central air.

Older homes often have a wall furnace, floor furnace, or baseboard heaters. Those heat the room they sit in and not much else. A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) moves heat instead of making it, so it can cover heating and cooling with one system and cut out the gas appliance.

## Ducted or ductless? How to choose

The big question in an older house is whether it has usable ductwork.

| Your home | Usually the better fit |
|---|---|
| Existing ducts in good shape | A ducted [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) |
| Wall or floor furnace, no ducts | [Ductless mini splits](/services/mini-splits) |
| ADU, garage conversion, or addition | A ductless mini split |
| Ducts that are leaky or undersized | Repair the ducts, or go ductless in problem rooms |

Ductless mini splits suit small Fairfax cottages and Ross Valley homes especially well. They heat and cool individual rooms quietly, and installation is far less invasive than running new ducts through an old house. If your ducts need work, our [duct work](/services/duct-work) page explains what we look at.

We size the system to the home, not to the label on the old unit. Insulation, windows, sun exposure, and the condition of any ducts all matter, and shaded canyon homes can differ a lot from a sunny house a few blocks away.

## What we check before moving off gas

Going without gas is mostly a planning job. Before we quote, we look at:

1. **The electrical panel.** Smaller, older panels are common here. Sometimes a new circuit is enough. Sometimes the panel needs an upgrade first.
2. **Where equipment can go.** Tight lots and hillside sites mean we think about outdoor unit placement, noise, and clearances up front.
3. **Access.** Crawlspaces, narrow stairs, and steep driveways affect how we work, and we plan for them.
4. **Hot water.** The gas water heater is usually the next gas appliance to go. A [heat pump water heater](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) can follow the heat pump, and planning both together saves surprises with the panel.

Every home is priced to your home after the free estimate, so you see clear written options before you decide anything.

## Common questions

### Can a mini split really heat a whole older home?

Yes, when it's sized and placed well. Some homes use one indoor unit in the main living area. Others use several units so bedrooms and offices heat on their own. We'll talk through what fits how you live.

### Will a heat pump keep my Fairfax or San Anselmo house warm in winter?

Yes. Our winters are mild, and modern inverter heat pumps are built for cold nights well below what we see here. Good insulation and sealed ducts help it work even better.

### Do I have to switch everything off gas at once?

No. Many homeowners start with heating and cooling, then add a heat pump water heater later. We plan the electrical side so the second step doesn't mean redoing the first.
