**Short answer:** Sonoma Clean Power is the local power provider for most of Sonoma County and Mendocino County, and it runs rebate programs for customers who switch to electric equipment like heat pumps and heat pump water heaters. Rebates are usually claimed after the work is done, the project needs a permit, and income-qualified customers may get more help. Amounts change, so check the official page before you plan around a number.

## Who can use Sonoma Clean Power programs?

Sonoma Clean Power is a community choice energy provider. It buys and supplies the electricity, while PG&E still delivers it and sends the bill. If your PG&E bill lists Sonoma Clean Power as your generation provider, you're a customer.

That covers most homes in towns like [Santa Rosa](/service-areas/santa-rosa), Petaluma, Rohnert Park, Windsor, Sebastopol, and Sonoma. If you opted out and buy your power straight from PG&E, Sonoma Clean Power's rebates may not apply to you, but other programs still might.

Marin homeowners are a different story. Most of Marin is served by MCE, which runs its own programs.

## What Sonoma Clean Power rebates can cover

Sonoma Clean Power's home programs focus on helping customers move off gas and use cleaner electricity. The equipment that comes up most in our work:

- **[Heat pumps](/services/heat-pumps) for heating and cooling.** One all-electric system that replaces an old furnace and an aging AC.
- **[Heat pump water heaters](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters).** These are one of the simplest gas-to-electric switches for a North Bay home, and Sonoma Clean Power has put a lot of attention on them.

Customers enrolled in the CARE or FERA bill discount programs have been eligible for enhanced rebates that cover more of the project cost. If you're on one of those programs, tell us at the estimate so we can factor it in.

We don't publish a dollar amount here, because a number in a blog post goes stale fast. For current amounts and rules, see the official [Sonoma Clean Power rebate page](https://sonomacleanpower.org/rebate-application).

## How the process works

Unlike some programs that run entirely through the contractor, Sonoma Clean Power rebates are typically applied for by the customer after the project is finished. That makes the paperwork on install day more important, not less. Here's how a project usually goes with Green Core:

1. **Free in-home estimate.** We look at your current equipment, your electrical panel, and where the new equipment will go.
2. **We check what's open.** Sonoma Clean Power is one of several programs we check, along with TECH Clean California and BayREN.
3. **Written options.** Each option is priced to your home, and we explain which equipment meets the program's efficiency requirements.
4. **Permit and install.** We pull the permit, do the work, and coordinate the final inspection. A permitted project is a program requirement.
5. **Your rebate packet.** We hand over what the application typically asks for: the paid invoice, the finaled permit, and equipment details like the model and nameplate photo.

Applications have a deadline after the project is complete, so don't let the paperwork sit in a drawer. Our post on [how Green Core handles rebate paperwork](/blog/how-green-core-handles-rebate-paperwork) goes into more detail.

## Stacking Sonoma Clean Power with other programs

Sonoma Clean Power is rarely the only option for a Sonoma County home. Here's how the main programs line up:

| Program | Who it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Sonoma Clean Power | Customers in its Sonoma and Mendocino County service area |
| TECH Clean California | A statewide program for heat pump HVAC and heat pump water heaters |
| BayREN | Homeowners across the nine Bay Area counties, including Sonoma |
| MCE | Customers in Marin County and its other member communities |

Some programs can be combined and some can't, and the rules depend on how each one is funded. That's why we look at them together when we quote your job instead of guessing. For more on the regional and statewide programs, see our posts on [BayREN home energy rebates](/blog/bayren-home-energy-rebates-sonoma-marin) and [TECH Clean California incentives](/blog/tech-clean-california-heat-pump-incentives).

The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025, so local programs like Sonoma Clean Power's matter more for 2026 projects.

## Tips so you don't miss a rebate

- **Don't buy equipment first.** Rebates depend on the equipment meeting efficiency rules. Let us confirm the model before anything is ordered.
- **Make sure it's permitted.** An unpermitted install can disqualify the project. We pull permits on every job.
- **Keep your paperwork together.** Invoice, permit sign-off, and equipment photos. We give you all of it at the end.
- **Check the electrical side early.** Some gas-to-electric switches need a new circuit or a panel check. We look at that during the estimate.
- **Don't wait on an emergency.** If your water heater is leaking, safety comes first. A failed tank can be replaced quickly, and a planned heat pump upgrade can still happen later.

## Common questions

### How do I know if I'm a Sonoma Clean Power customer?

Look at your PG&E bill. If Sonoma Clean Power is listed as your electric generation provider, you're a customer and can check its programs.

### How much is the Sonoma Clean Power rebate for a heat pump water heater?

It changes with the program, so we don't list a number here. The official Sonoma Clean Power rebate page shows current amounts, and we confirm what applies to your project at the estimate.

### Do I have to apply for the rebate myself?

Usually, yes. Sonoma Clean Power rebates are typically submitted by the customer after the work is done. We give you the invoice, permit, and equipment details the application asks for.

### Can I combine Sonoma Clean Power with TECH or BayREN?

Sometimes. It depends on each program's current rules. We check all of them together so you get the full picture before you decide.
