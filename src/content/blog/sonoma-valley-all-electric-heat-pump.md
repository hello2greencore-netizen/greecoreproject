**Short answer:** going all-electric in Sonoma Valley usually starts with a heat pump, because one system handles both heating and cooling and replaces your gas furnace. From there, the other pieces, like the water heater and the electrical panel, can follow at your pace. ## Why Sonoma Valley is a good fit for a heat pump

The valley has hot, dry summer afternoons and cool nights, with winters that are mild compared to much of the country. That swing is exactly what a [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) is built for. In summer it cools like a central air conditioner. In winter it reverses and moves heat into the house instead of burning gas.

For many homes in [Sonoma](/service-areas/sonoma), that means one system replacing an aging furnace and a separate air conditioner. It also means fewer things to maintain.

## Going all-electric in Sonoma Valley: the main pieces

"All-electric" is a few upgrades, not one big project. Most homeowners do them in order as equipment wears out.

| Piece | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Heating and cooling | A heat pump replaces the gas furnace and the AC |
| Water heating | A [heat pump water heater](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) replaces a gas tank |
| Electrical | A dedicated circuit for each new appliance, and sometimes a panel upgrade |
| Gas line | Capped by a licensed pro once nothing uses gas |

You don't have to do it all at once. If your furnace is the oldest thing in the house, start there. If the water heater is failing first, start there. We'll tell you honestly which order makes sense.

## Older Sonoma homes: ducts or no ducts?

Sonoma has historic homes near the Plaza, ranch houses, and newer builds out toward the Springs. Older homes often have little or no ductwork, tight crawlspaces, or a single wall furnace doing all the work.

- **Existing ducts in decent shape:** a ducted heat pump is usually the simplest swap. We check the ducts first, because leaky or undersized ducts waste a lot of what the system makes.
- **No ducts, or ducts in poor shape:** [ductless mini splits](/services/mini-splits) put compact indoor units in the rooms that need them. There is no need to open up walls.
- **A mix:** additions and converted spaces often do well with a mini split, while the main house keeps a ducted system.

We size the system to your home, not to the label on the old furnace. Insulation, windows, sun exposure, and duct condition all matter. See our [duct work](/services/duct-work) page if your ducts are a question mark.

## Electrical panel, permits, and rebates

Switching from gas usually needs a new dedicated circuit. Many homes can add one with no panel work, and some older panels need an upgrade first. We check this at the estimate so it doesn't surprise you later.

Permits depend on where you live. Inside the City of Sonoma limits, permits go through the city. In unincorporated areas like Boyes Hot Springs, El Verano, and Glen Ellen, they go through Permit Sonoma. We pull either one for you.

Most Sonoma Valley homes get their electricity through Sonoma Clean Power. Depending on program funding, rebates may be available through Sonoma Clean Power, TECH Clean California, and BayREN. Funding changes during the year, so we check what is open when we quote your job and handle the paperwork. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

## Common questions

### Can I go all-electric one step at a time?

Yes. Most people do. Replace the equipment that is wearing out first, and add the rest later. We plan the electrical so each step fits the next.

### Will a heat pump keep up on a hot valley afternoon?

Yes, when it is sized correctly and the ducts are in good shape. Modern inverter heat pumps are built for Sonoma County heat waves and cool winter nights.
