**Short answer:** a **tank water heater** is the lowest up-front cost and the simplest swap. A **tankless water heater** gives you endless hot water in a compact, wall-mounted unit. A **heat pump water heater** costs the least to run and usually qualifies for the best rebates. The right choice comes down to your budget today, your monthly bills, your space, and whether you plan to keep gas in the home.

If your water heater just quit, you probably don't want a lecture. So here's the quick version, the way we'd explain it standing in your garage.

## The three types of water heater, in plain words

**Tank (gas or electric).** A storage tank keeps a supply of water hot and ready, using either a gas burner or electric elements. It's the setup most North Bay homes already have. We install Bradford White and American Standard tanks.

**Tankless (gas).** No storage tank. A burner fires only when you open a tap and heats the water as it flows through. It mounts on a wall and never "runs out" as long as you stay within its flow rate. We install Navien condensing tankless units.

**Heat pump water heater.** It pulls heat from the surrounding air and moves it into a storage tank, like a refrigerator working in reverse. It's all-electric and the most efficient of the three. We install SANCO2 split systems and GE GeoSpring hybrid tanks.

## Which water heater fits your household?

**A tank makes sense if:**
- Your water heater failed and you need hot water back fast
- You want the lowest cost today and a like-for-like swap
- Your household's hot water use is steady and predictable

**Tankless makes sense if:**
- You run back-to-back showers, a big soaking tub, or several fixtures at once
- You want to free up floor space in a closet or garage
- You plan to stay on gas, and your gas supply can handle it

**A heat pump water heater makes sense if:**
- You want the lowest monthly bills over the life of the unit
- You have solar, or you're working toward an all-electric home
- You want to use rebates from TECH Clean California, Sonoma Clean Power, MCE, or BayREN, which may be available depending on where you live and current funding
- You have a garage, basement, or outdoor spot for the equipment

Our mild North Bay climate is a good match for heat pump water heaters. Garages in Petaluma or Santa Rosa rarely get cold enough to slow them down, and SANCO2 keeps its heat pump outside and keeps working well on chilly mornings.

## What changes when you switch types?

Swapping a tank for a tank is the simplest job. Changing types takes a bit more planning, and it's worth knowing up front:

1. **Tank to tankless.** Tankless units need more gas than a tank, so we check your gas line size and meter. They also need new venting, usually PVC.
2. **Gas tank to heat pump.** You'll need a dedicated electrical circuit, and we'll check whether your panel has room. The old gas line gets capped and the flue is no longer needed. Our post on [switching from a gas to an electric water heater](/blog/gas-to-electric-water-heater-what-changes) walks through it.
3. **Any new install.** California code calls for seismic straps, and many homes need an expansion tank. We handle both as part of the job.

We'll tell you about every one of these during the estimate, so nothing comes as a surprise on install day. That's the Ownership part of how we work: you get the full picture before you decide.

## What about rebates?

Tank and tankless water heaters rarely qualify for rebates. Heat pump water heaters often do, through programs like TECH Clean California, BayREN, Sonoma Clean Power, and MCE. Funding changes during the year, so we check what's open for your address when we quote the job and handle the paperwork for you. The federal 25C tax credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

## Common questions

### Which water heater is cheapest to install?

A standard gas or electric tank. With Green Core, tank replacements start at $2,850. Tankless and heat pump water heaters cost more up front and are priced to your home.

### Which water heater is cheapest to run?

A heat pump water heater. It moves heat from the air instead of burning gas or running electric elements, so it uses much less energy for the same hot water.

### Can I replace my tank with a different type the same day?

A like-for-like tank swap can often be done the same day when our schedule allows. Switching to tankless or a heat pump usually needs gas, venting, or electrical work, so we schedule it after the estimate.

### Do I really get endless hot water with tankless?

Yes, as long as you stay within the unit's flow rate. If too many fixtures run at once, the water can cool off, so we size the unit to how your household uses hot water.
