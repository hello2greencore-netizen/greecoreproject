**Short answer:** before you buy a home in Fairfield for a move to Travis AFB, check the age of the air conditioner, furnace, and water heater, how well the ducts deliver air to every room, and whether the work was permitted. Fairfield summers get hot, so the cooling system matters more here than in many places you may have lived. A quick HVAC check before closing tells you what you're inheriting and what to budget for.

## Why HVAC matters when you buy in Fairfield

Fairfield sits inland, and summer afternoons regularly get hot. The cooler marine air that keeps the coast comfortable often doesn't arrive until evening. A tired or undersized air conditioner can run all afternoon and still leave the house warm.

Winters are mild but nights get cold, so the furnace or heat pump still earns its keep. And because many PCS moves happen in summer, you may be moving in right when the AC is working its hardest.

A general home inspection is a good start, but it usually looks at whether the system turns on. It doesn't always tell you how much life is left, whether the house cools evenly, or whether the ducts are leaking into the attic.

## The Fairfield home buyer HVAC checklist

Use this list during showings and your inspection period. You can check most of it yourself.

1. **Find the age of each system.** The manufacture date is usually on the label of the outdoor AC or heat pump unit, the furnace, and the water heater. Write them down.
2. **Run the AC on a warm day.** Set it a few degrees below the room temperature and give it 15 minutes. Feel the air at vents upstairs and downstairs. It should feel noticeably cool in every room.
3. **Run the heat too.** Even in summer, switch to heat for a few minutes. Listen for banging, squealing, or a burning smell that doesn't fade.
4. **Check the upstairs.** In two-story homes, the upstairs is where cooling problems show up first. If the bedrooms feel stuffy, ask why.
5. **Look at the outdoor unit.** Bent fins, heavy rust, weeds growing into it, or a unit sitting crooked are signs of neglect.
6. **Look at the water heater.** Check for rust, water around the base, and seismic straps. California requires water heaters to be strapped.
7. **Pull the filter.** A filter caked with dust tells you how the system was maintained.
8. **Ask for records.** Service invoices, warranty paperwork, and permits for past replacements are all worth requesting from the seller.
9. **Note the thermostat.** Ask where it is and whether it controls one zone or two. Larger Green Valley and Cordelia homes sometimes have two systems.

## Red flags worth a closer look

Some findings aren't deal breakers, but they belong in your budget or your negotiation.

| What you find | What it may mean |
|---|---|
| AC or heat pump 15+ years old | Most air conditioners last around 15 to 20 years, so plan for replacement |
| Water heater over 10 years old | Most tank water heaters last about 8 to 15 years |
| Warm upstairs, cold downstairs | Duct leaks, weak return air, or a thermostat in the wrong spot |
| Very old system still running R-22 refrigerant | R-22 is no longer made, so some repairs get expensive |
| No permit for a recent replacement | Ask the seller; unpermitted work can complicate things later |
| Water stains near the furnace or water heater | A past or current leak to track down |

If the upstairs runs hot, it's often fixable without new equipment. Our post on [fixing uneven cooling in Fairfield two-story homes](/blog/upstairs-too-hot-two-story-homes-fairfield-green-valley) explains the usual causes.
