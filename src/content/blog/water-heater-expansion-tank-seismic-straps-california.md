An expansion tank is a separate part that absorbs extra pressure as water heats up, and many homes need one. If you live in Sonoma or Marin, you already know the ground here moves. A tank full of hot water that tips over is a real hazard, so these two small parts matter more than their size suggests.

## What are seismic straps, and does California require them?

Seismic straps are metal bands that anchor a tank water heater to the wall studs behind it. Their job is simple: keep the tank upright and attached to the wall during an earthquake, so it doesn't pull loose from its gas line, water lines, or vent.

California law requires water heaters to be braced or strapped. That applies to replacements and to existing tanks, not only new homes. Inspectors look for it when a replacement is permitted.

A properly strapped tank typically has:

- **Two straps**, one in the upper part of the tank and one in the lower part
- **Straps anchored into wall studs**, not just drywall
- **The lower strap kept clear of the controls**, so the gas valve or thermostat isn't squeezed
- **Flexible connectors** on the water lines (and the gas line for gas models), so the pipes can move without breaking

If your water heater sits free-standing in a garage with no straps, or only has a loose single strap, it's worth having someone look at it.

## What does a water heater expansion tank do?

Water expands when it heats. In an old plumbing system, that extra volume could push back into the city supply line. Many homes now have a one-way valve or pressure regulator at the main, which means the expanded water has nowhere to go.

That's where an expansion tank comes in. It's a small tank, usually mounted on the cold water line near the heater, with an air cushion inside that absorbs the extra volume. Without it, pressure can spike every time the heater runs, which stresses the tank, fittings, valves, and fixtures.

Signs a home may be missing a needed expansion tank include:

- A relief valve on the water heater that drips or discharges
- Banging or knocking pipes when the water heater fires
- Faucets or toilet fill valves that wear out early
- A water heater that fails sooner than you'd expect

None of these prove you need one, since other problems cause the same symptoms. A plumber or HVAC tech can check your water pressure and see whether the system is "closed."

## What we check at a water heater replacement

When we replace a tank, bracing and pressure aren't extras added at the end. They are part of doing the job right.

1. **Bracing.** We strap the new tank to the studs the way code requires.
2. **Connections.** We use flexible water connectors and check the gas line, venting, and shutoff on gas models.
3. **Pressure setup.** We look at whether your plumbing is a closed system and whether an expansion tank is needed or already installed. An existing one may need to be replaced or re-set for the new tank.

## How this applies to different water heater types

Straps and pressure protection apply across the board, with some differences in detail:

- **Gas and electric tanks.** Strapping is standard. See our [gas water heaters](/services/gas-water-heaters) and [electric water heaters](/services/electric-water-heaters) pages. Standard gas or electric tank replacement starts at $2,850.
- **Heat pump water heaters.** These are often taller and heavier, so bracing and placement need extra attention. See [heat pump water heaters](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters).
- **Tankless.** There's no storage tank to tip, so seismic straps matter less, though the unit still has to be mounted securely.

## Common questions

### Is a seismic strap kit enough on its own?

Only if it's installed correctly. The straps need to be anchored into studs at the right heights, with the controls left clear. A kit hung on drywall or with the wrong spacing can give you a false sense of safety.

### Do I need an expansion tank with every water heater?

Not always. It depends on whether your plumbing is a closed system, for example if you have a pressure regulator or check valve at the main. We check this at the estimate.

### Can I add straps to my existing water heater?

Often, yes. If your tank is in good shape, bracing it can be a small job. If it is also old and showing signs of failure, replacing it with a properly braced new unit may make more sense.

### Does the permit cover straps and the expansion tank?

A water heater replacement is permitted work, and inspectors look at the installation as a whole, including bracing. We pull the permit and coordinate the inspection for you.
