It's a good fit when your gas furnace and gas water heater are both getting old and you'd rather replace two gas appliances with one system. It isn't right for every home, so we evaluate yours before we install anything.

## What is Harvest Thermal?

Most homes have separate equipment for each job: a furnace or heat pump for heating, an AC for cooling, and a water heater in the garage or closet. Harvest Thermal combines those three jobs into a single heat pump system.

The "smart" part is the energy management. The system stores heat when electricity is cheapest and uses it when you need it. That means less of your heating and hot water load lands on expensive peak hours.

Green Core Heating & Air installs [Harvest Thermal](/services/harvest-thermal) systems in Sonoma and Marin counties, with completed projects in Petaluma and San Rafael.

## Why does one system matter in the North Bay?

Our mild winters and marine-layer mornings are easy on heat pumps, and inland summer afternoons are a job a properly sized system can handle. That makes the North Bay a natural place to move off gas.

The catch is that going all-electric in pieces can mean several projects over several years. Harvest Thermal is one way to do it in a single step, with one team doing the work and one system to learn. That's the Efficiency part of our CORE values: fewer separate machines, and a system designed around when power costs less.

## Who is Harvest Thermal right for?

It tends to make the most sense when several of these are true:

- **Both appliances are aging.** Your gas furnace and gas water heater are near the end of their lives, so you're facing two replacements anyway.
- **You want to go all-electric.** You're moving off gas and would rather do it in one project than two.
- **You're on a time-of-use electric rate.** The system is built to shift energy use to cheaper hours, so how your utility charges for power matters.
- **You like the idea of one system.** One installer, one set of controls, and one system to understand and maintain.

## When it may not be the right call

Harvest Thermal isn't a fit for every house, and we'd rather tell you that up front.

- If your furnace is fairly new and only the water heater is failing, a standalone [heat pump water heater](/services/heat-pump-water-heaters) is usually the simpler move.
- If you just need a like-for-like swap on a tank, a standard water heater replacement may be all you need.
- If your home has layout, space, or electrical limits, another option may work better. A [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) on its own, or a [ductless mini split](/services/mini-splits), can be a better match.

This is why we evaluate the home first. Insulation, ducts, space for equipment, and your electrical setup all factor in.
