**Short answer:** a room that is always too hot or too cold is usually an airflow problem, not a thermostat problem. Leaky or undersized ducts, an unbalanced system, or a room that loses or gains heat faster than the rest of the house are the most common causes. The fix depends on which one it is, so it pays to find out before you replace any equipment.

## Why does one room never feel right?

Your thermostat measures the temperature in one spot, usually a hallway. The system shuts off when that spot is comfortable, whether or not the back bedroom is. If one room gets less air, or loses heat faster, it falls behind every time.

Here are the usual suspects, roughly in the order we see them:

| Cause | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Leaky or disconnected ducts | Weak airflow at the vent, dusty or hot attic or crawlspace |
| Undersized or crushed duct to that room | Vent feels weak even when the system runs a long time |
| Long duct run | The room at the far end of the house always lags |
| Poor insulation or old windows | Room is fine in the morning, hot in the afternoon (or cold on windy nights) |
| Closed or blocked vents and returns | Doors closed, furniture over vents, no path for air to return |
| System size or design mismatch | Whole house struggles on the hottest and coldest days |

## Ducts: the most common culprit

Many older homes in Santa Rosa, Petaluma, Novato, and San Rafael have ducts that were installed decades ago. Over time, joints come loose, insulation falls off, and sections get crushed or pinched by other work in the attic or crawlspace.

When that happens, conditioned air leaks into spaces you're not trying to heat or cool. The room at the end of the line gets what's left.

A duct inspection can show whether sealing, a repair, or a replacement of one section is enough. Our [duct work](/services/duct-work) page covers what we do. Often a targeted fix costs far less than a new system and makes the whole house more even.

## Sun, insulation, and windows

Some rooms are hard to keep comfortable because of the room itself. A bedroom with big west-facing windows can bake on a summer afternoon, especially inland where heat waves hit hardest. A room over a garage or above a crawlspace can feel cold in winter because of what's under or next to it.

In those cases, more air isn't always the answer. Window shades, attic insulation, and sealing gaps can make a bigger difference than adjusting the system. We'll tell you when the problem is the room and not the equipment.

## When a mini split makes sense

Sometimes a room is just hard to reach with your existing ducts: an addition, a converted garage, a sunroom, or an ADU. Running new ductwork may not be practical.

A [ductless mini split](/services/mini-splits) gives that room its own heating and cooling, with its own thermostat, without touching the rest of the house. It's a common fix for one stubborn space, and it's a heat pump, so it heats in winter and cools in summer.

If the whole house struggles, a properly sized [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) with good ducts may be the better long-term answer. We size the system to the home, looking at insulation, windows, sun exposure, and duct condition, not just the label on the old unit.

## What you can try before calling

- Make sure vents are open and not blocked by furniture or rugs.
- Check that interior doors aren't sealing a room off from the return air path.
- Replace a dirty air filter, which restricts airflow to the whole house.
- Try the thermostat fan on "on" for a day to even out temperatures, and see if the problem room improves.

If none of that helps, the cause is probably in the ducts or the room itself, and it's worth having someone look.

## Common questions

### Why is my upstairs always hotter than downstairs?

Heat rises, and upstairs rooms are closer to a hot attic. A system that's balanced for the whole house can still leave upstairs behind, especially if the ducts in the attic are leaky or poorly insulated.

### Will a bigger air conditioner fix a hot room?

Usually not. A larger system can cool the thermostat area faster and shut off before the far room catches up. Fixing airflow to that room is typically a better answer than adding capacity.

### Can I close vents in rooms I don't use?

It's best not to close many. Closing vents can raise pressure in the ducts, increase leaks, and reduce airflow where you do want it. If a room is rarely used, we can talk about better options.

### How do I know if my ducts are the problem?

Weak airflow, dust, uneven temperatures, and high energy use are all clues. An in-home look at the ducts, attic, and crawlspace is the only way to know for sure.
