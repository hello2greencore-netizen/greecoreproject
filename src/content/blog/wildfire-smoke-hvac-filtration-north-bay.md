**Short answer:** during wildfire smoke, the best thing your HVAC system can do is recirculate indoor air through a clean, properly fitting filter with the windows and doors closed. A better filter, the fan running, and tight ducts all help keep smoke out of the rooms you live in. A system that is dirty, leaky, or pulling in outside air works against you.

Sonoma and Marin homeowners know the drill. When smoke settles over the North Bay, the goal is simple: keep the smoky air outside and clean the air that's already inside.

## How wildfire smoke gets into your home

Smoke comes in through open windows and doors, gaps around frames, attic and crawlspace vents, and leaky ductwork. Older North Bay homes tend to have more of these gaps than newer construction.

Your HVAC system moves a lot of air. If the ducts run through a smoky attic or crawlspace and have leaks, the system can pull that air in and spread it to every room. That is why duct condition matters in smoke season, not just in summer.

## Your filter is the first line of defense

The filter is the one part of the system you can check yourself in a few minutes. A few basics:

- **Check the MERV rating.** MERV is a rating printed on the filter frame. Higher numbers catch smaller particles. Basic fiberglass filters catch very little of the fine particles in smoke.
- **Make sure it fits.** A filter that is the wrong size leaves gaps, and air goes around it instead of through it.
- **Know your system's limit.** Very dense filters restrict airflow. On some systems that hurts comfort and efficiency. Before you move to a much higher-rated filter, ask a tech whether your system and filter slot can handle it.
- **Change it more often.** Filters load up quickly when the air is smoky. Check it during smoke events and replace it when it looks dark and clogged.

## Fan, recirculation, and fresh air settings

A few simple settings make a real difference:

1. **Close windows and doors** before smoke arrives, and keep them closed.
2. **Set the thermostat fan to ON** instead of AUTO so air keeps passing through the filter, even when the system isn't heating or cooling.
3. **Use recirculation, not fresh air intake,** if your system has a fresh air or ventilation option. Pulling in outside air during a smoke event brings the smoke with it.
4. **Skip the bathroom and kitchen fans** unless you need them. They pull air out of the house, which draws outside air in through every gap.

If you have a [heat pump](/services/heat-pumps) or [air conditioning](/services/air-conditioning) system, you can keep the house comfortable without opening a window. That matters on the days when a heat wave and smoke show up together. Ductless systems also recirculate indoor air, but each indoor unit has its own small filter that you'll want to keep clean.

## Leaky ducts make smoke season worse

If one part of the house always seems smokier or stuffier than the rest, the ducts may be a factor. Common trouble spots are disconnected joints, torn sections, and unsealed boots where the duct meets the register.

[Duct work](/services/duct-work) repairs and sealing can help keep unfiltered attic or crawlspace air out of your system. They also help with comfort and efficiency year-round, so they are worth looking at even if smoke is what got you thinking about it.

## What a stand-alone air cleaner can do

For a bedroom or main living area, a portable air cleaner with a true HEPA filter can add another layer. It won't replace a good HVAC filter, but the two work well together. Pick one rated for the size of the room, and run it with the door closed.

## Common questions

### Should I run my air conditioner during wildfire smoke?

Yes, if you need cooling. Keep windows closed and use the recirculation setting so the system cools the air already inside. Make sure the filter is clean first.

### Will a higher MERV filter always be better?

Not always. Higher-rated filters catch more fine particles, but a very dense filter can restrict airflow on some systems. Ask a tech whether your system can handle the filter you have in mind.

### Does a heat pump bring smoke indoors?

A standard heat pump recirculates your indoor air and does not bring outside air in. Smoke gets in through open windows, gaps, and leaky ducts instead, which is why sealing matters.

### How often should I change my filter during smoke season?

Check it every week or two while smoke is around. If it looks dark or clogged, replace it. A clogged filter hurts airflow and cleans less.
