Duct upgrades

Your ducts matter as much as the equipment

Ducts installed before the mid-2000s are often insulated to R-4.2 or less. California's current energy code calls for R-8 on new ducts in most of Sonoma, Napa and inland Marin, and R-6 closer to the coast.

Thin, leaky ducts in an attic or crawlspace lose heat in winter and pick up heat in summer before the air ever reaches your rooms. New, sealed, R-8 ducts are one of the best ways to improve how well any system performs and how much it costs to run.

We check your ducts on every home visit and tell you plainly whether they're worth replacing.

What size AC or heat pump does a Sonoma County home need?

Using this sizing check, a single-story 1,500 sq ft Sonoma County home built in the 1960s or 70s with its original ducts typically lands around 3.5 tons. A newer home of the same size with sealed ducts lands around 2 to 2.5 tons. Insulation, windows and ducts change the answer more than square footage does.

How the calculator works

We use your county's design temperatures, typical insulation for your home's age, your windows, air leaks and ducts, plus heat from people and appliances in summer. Then we pick real equipment sizes a bit above that load, the way we would on a proposal.

Why two quotes can differ

Different assumptions about insulation, windows, ducts and safety margins add up. Ask any contractor what they checked to size your system. A good one will walk you through it.

A planning guide for comparing quotes, not a final equipment selection. Design temperatures: ENERGY STAR county reference values (Sonoma 29°F / 96°F, Marin 32°F / 88°F, Napa 31°F / 98°F, San Francisco 40°F / 79°F). Coastal and hilltop homes can differ from the county figure.
